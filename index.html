About CRMO

A national Indigenous college protecting safe & ethical osteopathic care

The College of Registered Manual Osteopaths (Canada), based in Thornhill, Ontario, was registered as a national non-profit Indigenous organization on June 27, 2019. CRMO registers and supervises its members practising on treaty and tribunal lands of Indigenous Peoples, ensuring the public's right to safe and ethical manual osteopathic care is protected.

CRMO members are awarded the title of Registered Manual Osteopath (RMO). The title assures the health practitioner is a qualified manual osteopath who meets the requirements of a registered manual osteopath.

  • Employees, management and board are all Indigenous peoples (First Nation, Métis, Inuit), by blood or by adoption.
  • Only qualified health professionals are eligible for the title of RMO.
  • CRMO is not registered as a regulatory agency under provincial health regulatory authorities.

Osteopathy

Rooted in Indigenous bodywork & healing arts

Manual osteopathy has roots in traditional Indigenous medicine, bodywork and healing arts. Dr. Andrew Taylor Still, MD, DO — founder of osteopathy — lived on Shawnee Tribe reservation land for a decade and was introduced to Shawnee and Cherokee bodywork.

Techniques introduced by Dr. Still, such as osteoarticular techniques and soft tissue therapy, as well as cranial osteopathy (from which craniosacral therapy originated) and visceral manipulation, all have origins in Native American bodywork and medicine.

Reporting

Report a registered manual osteopath

All RMOs must adhere to the codes of ethics established by CRMO. If a practitioner has behaved in a manner that is malpractice, unethical or unauthorized, you can report them directly to the College.

01

Submit your report

Reports from the public and third-party payers — including extended health plan insurers — are accepted by mail or email.

info@manualosteopaths.org
02

Board investigation

CRMO's disciplinary board investigates every claim thoroughly and impartially, following the College's established code of ethics.

03

Public record

Reports of the disciplinary action are made public, so the community can see the College fulfilling its mandate.

Council of Elders

Guided by distinguished Indigenous hereditary chiefs

The Council of Elders provides cultural guidance, wisdom, leadership and advice to CRMO and its members — ensuring the perspectives and values of First Nations, Métis and Inuit communities are respected and represented within the College.

Land Acknowledgment

“The office of the College of Registered Manual Osteopaths (Canada) is located on the ancestral lands of the Wendat, the Haudenosaunee, and the Anishinaabe peoples, who have left their footprints on Mother Earth before us. We respectfully acknowledge those who have walked on it, those who walk on it now, and future generations who have yet to walk upon it.”

We wish to express gratitude to Mother Earth for the resources we are using, and honour all the Indigenous people who have been living on this land since time immemorial. We recommend that registered manual osteopaths post a land acknowledgement statement on their websites.
